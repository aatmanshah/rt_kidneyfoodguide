# Copy guidelines for kidneyfoodguide.com product pages

## Who reads these pages

Mostly people over 50 who live with chronic kidney disease (CKD), or who cook for someone who does. Many are
newly diagnosed and anxious about food. They are not in a hurry, they read carefully, and many are on phones.
They have been burned by internet health claims before. Plain, warm, specific copy converts better here than
hype.

## Voice

- Warm and practical, like a friend who is a good cook and knows the renal diet. Not clinical, not salesy.
- Lead with the feeling the product gives back ("cozy fall flavors", "enjoy food again"), then the specifics.
- Specific beats vague: "33 recipes across 6 categories" beats "dozens of delicious recipes".
- Short sentences. One idea per paragraph. Headlines in Title Case like the existing pages.

## Health and medical wording (important)

These are food-education products, not treatment. Writing that crosses into medical claims creates real legal
and trust risk.

- OK: "kidney-friendly", "renal-diet friendly", "lower in sodium, potassium and phosphorus", "built with
  kidney-safe swaps", "without the sodium, potassium or phosphorus guesswork".
- Not OK: "cures", "treats", "reverses", "prevents dialysis", "improves your GFR/kidney function", "doctor
  approved" (unless true and documented), guaranteed outcomes, or specific numbers about health results.
- Keep the footer line: "Educational content only — not a substitute for medical advice from your care team."
- Add an FAQ answer that suggests checking with their dietitian or nephrologist, because needs differ by CKD
  stage and dialysis status.
- Don't invent nutrition numbers. Only use figures that are in the product itself.

## Facts and trust

- Every number on the page (recipe counts, categories, pages) must match the product. Ask or read the PDF.
- The partner logo strip (ASN, KidneyX, EIT Health, HHS CTO) and the "About RenalTracker" block are shared
  company content: reuse them unchanged. Don't add new endorsements.
- No fake urgency (countdown timers, "only 3 left" for a PDF), no invented testimonials. Real testimonials
  only if the user provides them.

## Page sections (in this order)

1. **Hero**: badge (e.g. "New seasonal digital cookbook"), H1 promise, a one-paragraph lede naming what it is,
   buy button + "See What's Inside", a delivery note ("Instant digital download · Delivered right after
   checkout").
2. **Product facts**: four big-number facts, then the shared partner logos.
3. **The problem**: why this exists, plus 2 or 3 small cards naming real frustrations.
4. **What's inside**: one card per chapter or category with counts and a couple of named items.
5. **A peek inside**: 1 or 2 real page previews (skip the section if there are none).
6. **About RenalTracker**: the shared block.
7. **FAQ**: what's included; digital vs physical; how delivery works (the download is on the page right
   after checkout); can I print it; is it right for my stage (talk to your care team); contact.
8. **Final CTA**: cover, short headline, one line, buy button, delivery note.

## Buttons

Use one consistent verb phrase across all buy buttons on a page ("Get the Recipes", "Get the Guide"). Don't
use "Buy now"; the existing pages avoid it.
