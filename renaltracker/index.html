How it works

Four steps from questions to grocery list.

It takes a few minutes. Only the first two steps are required.

  1. 1

    Health profile

    Choose your CKD stage: Stage 1-2, Stage 3, Stage 4-5 not on dialysis, Dialysis, or Unsure. Say whether you also have diabetes and/or high blood pressure.

  2. 2

    Dietary baseline

    Set servings and plan length (3 or 7 days). Pick vegetarian, vegan or no seafood, and list ingredients you dislike.

  3. 3

    Optional context

    If you want, add recent lab hints such as your potassium level or a daily sodium cap, and any notes. Never required.

  4. 4

    Instant output

    Get a personalized meal plan with estimated carbs, fiber, protein, sodium, potassium and phosphorus per meal, and a grocery checklist to tick off in the store aisle.

Features

Less guesswork at the kitchen table and in the store.

  • Meal plans with kidney-aware estimates

    Every meal shows estimated carbs, fiber, protein, sodium, potassium and phosphorus, so you can see the numbers that matter to kidney eating at a glance.

  • Smart grocery checklist

    One list for the whole plan, grouped by store department. Tick items off as you walk the aisles.

  • Kidney, diabetes and blood pressure together

    Advice for these conditions can pull in different directions. RenalTracker balances them in one plan, and when they conflict, kidney limits take priority.

  • Remembers your profile

    Your answers are saved, so next week's plan is one tap instead of starting the questionnaire again.

  • Works on your phone

    Designed for mobile first, and you can add it to your iPhone home screen for quick access in the store.

Built with safety in mind

What that means, and what it does not.

RenalTracker is a planning aid. It does not replace your doctor or renal dietitian.

  • Kidney limits always come first

    If kidney guidance conflicts with diabetes or blood pressure guidance, the plan follows the kidney limits.

  • Plans are AI-generated estimates

    Meals and nutrition numbers are generated by AI. They are estimates, they can be wrong, and they have not been reviewed by a dietitian.

  • Your care team has the final say

    Check any plan with your doctor or renal dietitian before you change how you eat, especially if you take medicines or have recent lab results to consider.

  • You stay in control

    You choose what to share. Lab hints and notes are optional, and you decide what to cook and buy.
